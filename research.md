My research sits in **organic chemistry** and has two connected themes. The first is the **discovery and characterization of natural products** from medicinal plants and food crops. The second is the **synthesis of small organic molecules**, often inspired by natural product scaffolds, as candidates with useful biological activity.

## 1. Natural Product Chemistry

Plants make a wide range of secondary metabolites with valuable biological properties. My group extracts, isolates, and characterizes these compounds and evaluates their bioactivity.

**Focus areas**

- **Flavonoids and isoflavonoids.** We study their structural diversity, isolation, and pharmacological properties in medicinal plants such as *Rumex nervosus*, *Thymus schimperi*, and *Millettia* species.
- **Triterpenoids and saponins.** We study the phytochemistry and pharmacology of saponin-rich plants such as *Phytolacca dodecandra* (Endod).
- **Bioactive metabolites in food crops.** We profile flavonoids, isoflavones, anthocyanins, and other polyphenols in soybean, mung bean, faba bean, and sorghum germplasm, and examine how genotype and seed traits affect them.
- **Biological evaluation.** We test isolated compounds and extracts for antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-proliferative activity.

**Approach:** extraction and purification of biomolecules, followed by identification and quantification of metabolites using chromatographic and mass spectrometric methods (LC-ESI-MS/MS, GC-MS).

## 2. Synthesis of Small Organic Molecules

Natural products often give us useful starting scaffolds for medicinal chemistry. We design and synthesize small organic molecules, characterize them, and evaluate their biological activity.

**Focus areas**

- **Chalcones and related flavonoid-type scaffolds.** We design, synthesize, and characterize chalcone derivatives and evaluate their activity *in vivo*, for example antidiabetic activity.
- **Natural-product-inspired design.** We use structural features of bioactive flavonoids and isoflavonoids to guide the design of new synthetic analogues.
- **Structure–activity relationships.** We link structural changes in synthetic molecules to differences in their biological activity.
